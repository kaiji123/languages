The Italian phrase **"una cosa"** literally means **"a thing"** in English.

Here’s a quick breakdown:
- **una** = a (feminine)
- **cosa** = thing

**How it's used:**

1. **Literal:** referring to an object, matter, or fact.
   - *"Ho visto una cosa strana."* = "I saw a strange thing."

2. **In common expressions:**
   - *"Che cosa?"* = "What?" (literally "What thing?")
   - *"Una cosa è certa..."* = "One thing is certain..."
   - *"È una cosa importante."* = "It's an important thing/matter."

3. **As a filler or softener** (similar to "something" or "stuff" in English):
   - *"Vorrei dirti una cosa."* = "I'd like to tell you something."

**Note:** In informal spoken Italian, *"cosa"* alone (without *una*) can also mean "what" or "thing," but *"una cosa"* always means **"a thing"** or **"one thing."**
